Voice

Four traits, always in the room.

Written in second person, direct to the homeowner. These four hold regardless of which channel or campaign is speaking.

Intentional

Measured and clear, speaking with purpose without unnecessary jargon.

  • Do: ensure every sentence is doing a job
  • Do: use measured language
  • Avoid: overselling

Lively

Optimistic, up-tempo, and forward-moving enthusiasm in service of action.

  • Do: maintain an up-tempo pace
  • Do: focus on getting things done
  • Avoid: superficial hype

Knowledgeable

Smart, assured, and grounded expertise that never patronizes.

  • Do: speak with logical confidence
  • Do: give expert-level insight without condescension
  • Avoid: guessing

Approachable

Straightforward, honest, and human, balancing optimism with reality.

  • Do: use accessible language
  • Do: speak like you are talking to a neighbor
  • Avoid: corporate-speak

Invariants, never removable by campaign tone

  • Never use fear-based framing
  • Always be proactive, not reactive
  • Maintain a measured and honest tone that is never oversold

Mechanics

How the sentences get built.

Sentence structure

Short, declarative sentences that let the idea land before moving on.

Vocabulary

Grounded and logical. Avoids insurance jargon like “loss ratio” or “peril” unless the context is expert-specific.

Structural habits

Direct address to the homeowner, and translation of technical terms for non-experts.

Formatting

Avoid exclamation points, ALL CAPS emphasis, and stacked adjectives.

Storytelling

Focus on confidence and freedom rather than fear, using concrete specifics to build warmth.

Distinctive marker

Contrast structures. Not X. Y instead.

Language

Prefer this. Avoid that.

Prefer

proactive 70+ carriers 60 seconds 500K+ homeowners

Avoid

best always never guaranteed loss ratio peril endorsement "We are pleased to announce" "In today's fast-paced world"

Examples

Sounds like vs. does not sound like.

The clearest way to hear the voice is side by side with what it isn't.

Service description

Sounds likeWe help you find the coverage that fits your home.
Does not sound likeTrust us to protect everything you own.

Be specific and helpful rather than making vague, sweeping promises.

Value proposition

Sounds likeFewer incidents. Fewer claims. Less worry.
Does not sound likeGuaranteed to eliminate all your home insurance headaches.

Use measured, realistic benefits instead of absolute guarantees.

Speed / efficiency claims

Sounds likeGet quotes from 70+ carriers in under 60 seconds.
Does not sound likeThe fastest, cheapest insurance on the market — period.

Use concrete data points rather than aggressive superlatives.

Brand identity

Sounds likeWe're the proactive insurance group for today's homeowners.
Does not sound likeHippo is revolutionizing the entire insurance industry.

Focus on the customer's needs rather than corporate grandiosity.

Using this profile

The source of truth for Hippo copy sign-off.

Every public sentence, sales email, claims FAQ, or product description, should trace back to these four traits and these boundaries. When a draft feels off, the fix is usually to cut the hype and get specific.
