What Claude thinks you are

"What is EVCO Insurance?" — asked of the AIs

When we asked Claude to describe EVCO by name, it had to search the web to answer — then correctly returned: a Walnut Creek, California employee benefits brokerage and HR-technology firm, founded in 2005, that helps small and mid-sized businesses shop and manage health benefits alongside HR and compliance tools. On the site: "benefits made simple," 36,000+ lives under management, two decades of experience, the national benefits platform for Community Health Centers via NACHC Cares, plus M&A benefits support, paperless onboarding, payroll sync and one-click COBRA. Google already recognizes the brand — EVCO owns a Knowledge Panel and organic position #1 on its own name.

So the foundation is real: EVCO owns its brand search, has a Knowledge Panel, a LinkedIn page, clean metadata, and a library of genuinely substantial posts (1,000+ words each). The problem is narrower and fixable. When we asked the questions your buyers actually type — with no company name attached — EVCO surfaced nowhere; the answers went to Gusto, Rippling, Justworks, Marsh McLennan and Hub International. An independent 5-engine probe (DataForSEO, Sept 12) found the same thing: cited 0 out of 80 times. Two specific reasons AI can't cite you: the technical signals aren't there (no schema/structured data, no llms.txt, no FAQ markup, no Wikidata or Crunchbase entity), and the strong content you do publish is thought-leadership on renewals and planning — not the comparison and "best broker for X" pages buyers actually ask AI about. Being findable-by-name is not the same as being recommended, and recommendations are what buyers act on.
