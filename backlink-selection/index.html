Backlink selection pipeline

Candidate retrieval, relevance gating, and coverage.

Three stages run in sequence for every blog draft: a vector-similarity search retrieves scored candidates from the portfolio’s article catalog, a relevance gate filters those candidates down to genuine matches, and a coverage calculation determines how many survivors are actually inserted.

In short

A candidate that fails a gate is dropped, not force-included. A self-links-only result is a valid, correct output when no cross-venture candidate clears every gate.

Stage 1

Vector similarity search over the portfolio’s article catalog.

Every candidate article is embedded once (title plus full body, truncated to an 8,000-token cap) and stored against its venture in the shared backlink graph. At generation time, the current blog brief is embedded once and scored against every stored vector.

semantic       = 1 − cosine_distance(brief_vector, article_vector)
recency        = max(0, 1 − days_since_published / 730)
combined_score = (1 − w) × semantic + w × recency   // w = 0.15

embedding      = text-embedding-3-small (1536-d)

Retrieval filters

  • content_kind: must equal 'article'
  • http_status: must equal 200
  • Link history: not previously linked
  • Venture: within the allowed set

Ranking

  • Pool filter: landing and service pages are excluded, since their broad context summaries otherwise out-rank topical articles on pure similarity.
  • Top slice: ranked by combined_score, the top 15 are retained.
  • Per-venture floor: ⌈15 ÷ N⌉ guarantees every allowed venture surfaces at least one candidate, so a single high-scoring venture can’t crowd out the rest.
Stage 2

Three checks applied to every retrieved candidate, in order.

Each candidate that survives retrieval is judged against the requesting venture on industry, sub-industry, and topic, in that order, by the same model used for content generation. A candidate must clear a check to be evaluated on the next one. Failing any check removes it from the pool.

Gate 1

Industry / vertical relevance

For each candidate, industry/vertical and primary buyer are classified from its title and summary, not its URL, and compared against the requesting venture’s own industry and audience. A candidate advances only if it operates in the same industry/vertical, or serves the same buyer persona in a genuinely overlapping market.

Rule

A shared business function or theme (staffing, cost, compliance, growth) is not sufficient on its own. Industry mismatch is a hard drop, decided independently of how well the specific topic lines up.

Gate 2

Sub-industry relevance (NAICS)

Candidates that clear the industry check are compared at a finer grain, using NAICS classification, the standard code system for a company’s specific line of work. Two ventures can share an industry while serving very different sub-industries.

Passes

  • Matching NAICS code family, for example industrial or temporary staffing.

Rejected

  • Same top-level industry, different code family, for example executive search.
Gate 3

Topic relevance

Only candidates that clear gates 1 and 2 are evaluated here. A shared surface word (“cost,” “scaling,” “automation”) is not treated as a bridge on its own. The test applied: would this brief’s reader, in their specific role and decision, actually click through and find the destination genuinely on-point?

Rule

Tactical-execution content within the same sub-industry (for example “how to measure X”) can bridge for a leadership- or pricing-focused brief about a domain leader. That clarification does not reopen a candidate already dropped at gate 1 or 2.

Stage 3

How many backlinks are actually inserted.

Selection is relevance-gated, not force-covered. The count of backlinks inserted is bounded by formula, but never padded with a candidate that failed a gate.

min_count = min(2, total_available)
max_count = min(8, max(4, round( 0.15 × total_available )))

Every candidate in the retrieved pool must land in exactly one of two buckets: selected (mapped to a specific URL and anchor sentence) or dropped (logged with a reason, tagged to the gate it failed). A count that can’t be met with genuine matches is not backfilled with a weaker candidate.

The pipeline retries up to 3 times per brief, and each retry targets only the candidates left unaccounted for, not the full pool. The attempt with the fewest unaccounted candidates is kept.

Rule

If zero cross-venture candidates clear all three gates, a self-links-only result is the correct output for that brief, not a fallback or a failure state.
