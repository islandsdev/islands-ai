How we choose what to write

Every post is an expansion, a gap, or a trend.

Three buckets, running in parallel. Expansion compounds what you already have proof for. Gap takes ground a competitor is holding. Trend puts you in front of demand before it peaks. Roughly 40 / 40 / 20 by volume.

Expansion

Own what you have already earned

You have four verified, citable proof points and zero articles built on them. This is the cheapest visibility in the plan because the substance already exists.

  • Moisture-first construction, explained from the restoration work that produced it
  • Essential versus Diamond, line by line, with what each tier includes
  • How a fixed itemized quote is built and why numbers move on other bids
  • How the $5,000 on-time guarantee actually works
  • Completed project walkthroughs from the 200-plus GTA builds
Gap

Take ground competitors are holding

Thirteen rows where a rival is placed and Prime is absent. These are winnable because the incumbents are ranked but weak, mostly page-two positions and thin pages.

  • City pages: Mississauga, Vaughan, Etobicoke, Scarborough, North York, Brampton
  • Comparison pages against all five named competitors
  • Cost of a basement renovation in Toronto, currently held by an AI Overview citation that is not yours
  • Best basement builders in the GTA, where two rivals place and you do not
Trend

Get in front of where demand is moving

The surfaces buyers use are shifting faster than the keywords are. This bucket is the reason the plan is not just a blog.

  • Legal secondary suites and basement apartments, the fastest-moving question in GTA below-grade work
  • Answer-engine formats: front-loaded answers, FAQ schema, question-shaped headings
  • Underpinning versus bench footing, and when each is genuinely required
  • Basement ROI and rental math for investor-owners
