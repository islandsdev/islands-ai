You asked what we are targeting to reach the top of AI answer engines, and whether to niche down. Yes, niche down. But on question specificity, not subject area. Here is the reasoning and what changes in the October roadmap.

What changed

The second roadmap targets absence, not volume

The topics now in production are selected on a different signal. Not how many people search the term, but whether any AI engine currently has a source it considers good enough to cite.

  • Compare residential proxy pricing for web scrapingAbsent from 5 of 5 engines
  • Cheapest residential proxies per GB for small scale scrapingAbsent from 5 of 5 engines
  • How to handle anti-bot systems when scraping real estate listingsAbsent from 4 of 4 engines
  • How do residential proxies prevent IP bans during data collectionAbsent from 4 of 5 engines

Five engines, asked a direct commercial question by a buyer with a budget, and not one has a source it will cite. That is not a keyword. It is a vacancy.

Why it works

Answer engines do not rank pages. They assemble answers.

Ranking is a long game

To rank, a page competes on authority and link equity. That takes years and favours whoever started first.

Citation is not

To be cited, a page has to be the most specific credible source for the exact question asked. A narrow page with one clear answer beats a broad page on a stronger domain.

MrScraper's own result proves it. The strongest AI Overview win so far came on "Agentic vs Deterministic Web Data Workflows." Google used MrScraper's own framing, "do you know the exact execution path before the job starts," to open its answer, and placed MrScraper above Reddit in the source panel. Two days after publish.

That page did not win on authority. It won because it was the clearest articulation of a specific question. Perplexity, at 7 of 16 prompts, remains the strongest engine and the best early signal that a page is structurally citable.

In practice

What niching down actually means

  1. Target prompts, not keywords

    A prompt is the full question a buyer types into an assistant. Longer, more specific, far less contested than the keyword inside it.

  2. Go where the absence is

    20 of 45 tracked targets have nobody in the top 10. Cheaper to win than anything with an incumbent, and several map directly to the product.

  3. Pick the vertical

    The highest-priority topics cluster around real estate listings, recruitment data and e-commerce pricing. Vertical questions are where the vacancies sit, because generalist competitors write generalist content.

  4. Front-load the answer

    Every page opens with 40 to 60 words that answer the question outright and can be lifted verbatim. Highest-leverage structural change available, now standard on everything we ship.

  5. One claim per page nobody else has published

    Engines cite what they cannot get elsewhere. A benchmark, a cost comparison, an original measurement. Restating the category wins nothing.

One item to resolve

A title in the current batch needs changing

Flagged before publish

Topic 3 in the batch now in production is titled "Bypassing Anti-Bot Systems with Smart Residential Proxy Rotation." It carries the highest priority score in the batch and is slotted for Week 2.

That runs against the repositioning brief we were given three weeks ago, which moved us away from bypass and detection-evasion language in light of the Google and SerpApi case.

The underlying target is sound. The prompt is genuinely absent from 4 of 4 engines and maps to a real capability. Only the framing needs to change.

We also found the ICP field inside both roadmaps still reads "gain market insights without being blocked by anti-bot systems." That field feeds every brief the generator produces, so the framing reproduces itself into new topics automatically. We are correcting it at source rather than per article.

Recommendation: keep the target, retitle to the reliability framing, fix the ICP field. No loss of search coverage, and the compliance position holds.
