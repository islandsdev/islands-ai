Blog performance

The blog drew 20,679 views from 11,193 active users over the period, at 1.85 views per user and an average engagement time of 18 seconds. Engagement rate was 58.79%, bounce rate 41.21%. Traffic reached 204 separate pages.

Measured against the whole site, the blog accounted for 13.73% of all views and 29.18% of all active users. Close to a third of everyone who arrives on fuelfinance.me now reaches a blog page.

02 The pattern worth building on

The page AI engines cite is the page that converts

The blog recorded 4 key events over the period. Three of those 4 came from one page, and it is the same page Google’s AI Overview cites.

01

One page carries the conversions

AI search visibility

Ahrefs records 241 AI responses across 50 pages in the trailing quarter. The movement is uneven by engine, and the direction matters more than the totals.

Perplexity is the clearest gain of the period. It rose from 8 responses at the start of the programme to 49, across 16 cited pages, climbing in all but one week of the engagement.

ChatGPT moved off zero for the first time. One citation is a small number, but it is the engine where Fuelfinance had no presence at all when measurement began.

Google’s own AI surfaces declined over the same window. AI Overviews and AI Mode draw on the classic search index, so their movement tracks the ranking change below rather than running independently of it.

Of 1,597 referring domains, 506 are followed, at 31.7%. Of 3,665 backlinks, 1,534 are followed, at 41.9%. The majority of the link growth carries no ranking weight, so the headline referring domain figure overstates its effect on position.

Google Search Console records 2,050 clicks from 1,250,000 impressions across the period, a 0.2% click-through rate at an average position of 19.8. High impressions against low clicks is the expected shape where AI Overviews answer the query on the results page.

Next step

Extend what already works

The pages that earn AI citations and the pages that convert are currently the same pages. The next quarter means more comparison and original-data content in that shape, and a commercial layer against the transactional terms the footprint does not yet cover.
