SEO timelines

How long SEO actually takes, according to Google

These are Google’s own figures for how long each step of search takes, from first crawl to a recovered ranking. We share them so timelines are set honestly: good SEO compounds, but it does not land overnight.

In short

Google publishes rough timings for everything its crawler and index do. Some steps take hours. Others take weeks or months. A fresh page can be discovered in about a day, but recrawling, migrations and core-update recovery run on their own clock. Plan the work, then give it room to take effect.

Timelines

Results follow Google’s clock, not the launch date

These latencies are structural. They are built into how crawling, indexing and serving work, and no vendor removes them.

The honest plan is to ship the right changes, then measure against these windows instead of expecting movement the next morning. A new page can be found within a day. A title or description change can show within a day or two. Bigger moves, a migration, a canonical change, recovery from a core update, run on weeks or months.

Use the figures below to see which part of the process a given result depends on. When you know whether a change lives in crawling, indexing or serving, the wait makes sense, and the work gets planned around it rather than against it.
