03 — Live defects

Eight things shipping right now

All directly observed on the live site. Ranked by severity. The first four are hours of work, and several are pure data entry.

1
Broken template tag on all five lesson pages

Every /lessons/[city] page renders an unresolved {location} placeholder in visible copy, with a generic sitewide title, no meta description and no structured data. Broken text is reaching both customers and Google.

2
Empty phone and map fields in your schema

telephone and hasMap are blank on all seven location pages. This is the single biggest local-search gap you have: "near me" results are assembled from exactly these fields.

3
Structured data points at a URL that doesn't resolve

The JSON-LD url field references /location/ (singular) while pages serve from /locations/ (plural). You are telling engines your canonical page is a broken link. Broken markup is worse than no markup, because engines act on what they read.

4
Two Glendale pages sharing one address

Your newest and best-funded location is split against itself. Consolidate to one canonical page, 301 the other, and keep the "formerly Desert Mirage" landmark content on the survivor.

5
Both blog posts missing title tags entirely

The title exists only as an on-page heading. This is why a crawler sees almost no blog content despite daily publishing.

6
Shoal Creek collides with an unrelated golf course

The page competes with the public Shoal Creek Golf Course and is addressed as Kansas City, MO. In our citation probe, Perplexity confused it with Shoal Creek in Birmingham, Alabama. Engines cannot tell the three apart.

7
Conflicting Ranch Pass pricing

/ranch-pass and /new-ranch-pass disagree with each other. Conflicting facts on your highest-intent commercial page.

8
The same three testimonials on all six location pages

Julie Romero, Tommy Le and Kyle Koch appear verbatim on every location. Duplicate content, at exactly the moment a thin-content update is rolling.

04 — The traffic drop

Three causes, not one

Organic sessions down roughly 65%, Search Console clicks down roughly 15%. The start date is reported as 23 June by your team and around 28 June in our tracking. That six-day discrepancy matters and should be pinned down first.

1. Measurement

In July we consolidated two competing GA4 properties, removed duplicate tags that were inflating pageviews, fixed cross-domain tracking and excluded internal test traffic. Every one of those fixes reduces the reported number without a single visitor going anywhere. A 50-point gap between two metrics measuring nearly the same thing is unlikely to be all real traffic loss.

2. Google's June 2026 spam update

Google's status dashboard confirms a spam update rolling out 23 to 24 June and completing 26 June, targeting thin, unoriginal and scaled content. Your exposure is specific: five broken template pages, fifteen indexed thank-you pages, a duplicate Glendale page, and three testimonials repeated across six locations. That is the profile these updates target. A core update running 21 May to 2 June may also have been building instability beforehand.

3. The URL restructure reaching production early

The site is currently serving location pages from /locations/ while its own structured data still points at /location/, and the sitemap has lost thirteen URLs inside a week. Older lesson-page paths (/lessons-coaches/, /lesson/) are still surfacing in search snippets alongside the current ones.

The first question to answer

Phase 1 of the restructure was scheduled to sit on staging, with internal linking and redirect migration following on 14 and 15 August. The evidence on the live site suggests the new hierarchy is already in production, ahead of its redirects.

If that is what happened, it explains the template bug, the structured data pointing at dead URLs, the shrinking sitemap, the fragmented lesson-page history, and a meaningful share of the traffic decline, all at once. Confirm this with the dev team before anything else in this document gets actioned.

These three are not mutually exclusive. The likeliest reading is that all three contributed, in roughly that order of size.

  • Plot Search Console clicks against GA4 sessions from 1 June, marked with the date each tracking fix went live.

05 — Root causes

Five things suppressing local discovery

1. Google cannot place your six locations

No published phone number or street address anywhere on the site or in Google's knowledge graph. Empty schema fields. No Knowledge Panel on a branded search, no Wikidata record, no company LinkedIn page, no Crunchbase entry. For a six-site physical business this is the highest-severity structural finding in the audit, and the most plausible reason you sit at position 21 on a 60,500-a-month term you should own in six metros.

2. Structured data exists but is broken

Location pages carry SportsActivityLocation markup with its most important fields empty and its canonical URL pointing nowhere. Elsewhere there is nothing: no FAQPage despite genuinely good FAQ content on the homepage, /ranch-pass and /toptracer-tech; no Event markup despite the Golf Ranch Classic and the 2v2 leagues; no Offer markup despite bucket pricing. This is the cheapest fix in the document with the widest blast radius, because you already have the content these tags describe.

07 — The strategy

Six local businesses that share a brand

Golf Ranch is currently built as one national website that mentions six cities. It should be built as six local businesses that share a brand, a template and a domain. This is the central recommendation of the audit.

Nobody searches for a national driving range chain. They search for somewhere to hit balls tonight, twenty minutes from where they are standing. Every ranking signal that decides that search — proximity, Business Profile completeness, local reviews, address consistency, city-specific content — is scored per location, not per brand. A single blended site cannot compete on any of them, which is exactly what the data shows.

Architecture: one domain, six city hubs

/glendale-az/                    ← canonical location home
   /glendale-az/lessons/          instructors, pricing, booking
   /glendale-az/clinics/          Women's 101, Men's 101, 102 series
   /glendale-az/ranch-pass/       membership, local pricing
   /glendale-az/events/           corporate, leagues, the Classic
   /glendale-az/free-bucket/      local offer, local form

/grand-prairie-tx/   /richardson-tx/   /lees-summit-mo/
/shoal-creek-mo/     /brookfield-ct/

Subfolders, not subdomains, and not six separate sites. Every link, mention and citation Golf Ranch has ever earned accrues to one domain. Six separate domains, or six subdomains, would each start from zero authority and each need their own link-building programme. With organic already under pressure, splitting the domain would be self-inflicted damage. Subfolders let Glendale inherit the authority Brookfield earned while still giving each city its own indexable, locally-optimised home. It is also reversible; a domain split is not.

Sequencing matters here

Because a restructure appears to be mid-flight already, finish and stabilise the current migration before starting this one. Two overlapping URL changes with incomplete redirects is how a site loses another 30%.

13 — Method & caveats

How this was measured

Full site inventory, technical signals, structured data and entity footprint captured by crawl and direct HTML inspection in the first week of August 2026. Citation results are measured, not modelled: 22 buyer questions run against ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Gemini and Claude — 16 branded, producing 55 cited-with-link results across 80 question-engine pairs, and 6 non-branded category questions, of which one surfaced Golf Ranch. Search volumes are 12-month US averages. Positions are Google top-30; a dash means not placed in the top 30. The visibility score weights SEO at 25%, GEO at 35% and AEO at 40% across nineteen signals.

Flagged rather than asserted

  • Whether the lessons template bug is server-side, and so visible to Googlebot, or a client-side issue the renderer resolves differently. A JS-rendering crawl or Search Console will settle it.
  • Index counts and historical ranking trend. A site: query was blocked by robots restrictions and archive lookups returned no usable date, so Search Console Coverage is the fastest path.
  • Estimated traffic or pipeline value. Keyword-aggregator quota was exhausted mid-audit, and we would rather present nothing than an unverified number.
  • The "1,500+ five-star reviews" claim, which is currently sourced only from your own homepage. Third-party platforms show 18 to 25 reviews per location where found, and no review data at all for Lee's Summit or Glendale. The aggregate figure may well be right, but it should not go into AI-facing content until it can be sourced, because a proof point an engine cannot corroborate becomes a liability.
  • The traffic decline itself, until Search Console and Analytics are reconciled as described in section 4.
