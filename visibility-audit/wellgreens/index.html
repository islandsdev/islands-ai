What we found

Six gaps, in priority order.

None of these is a strategy problem. Each one is specific and fixable, and the first two are quick wins your team can start this week.

01 · Profile namesHigh risk

10 of 10

profiles carry keywords in the business name.

Every profile is named “Wellgreens [City] Weed Cannabis Dispensary.” Google’s guidelines require the name to match your real-world storefront and rule out product, service and location details.

As shown in Google Maps: Wellgreens La Mesa - Lake Murray Rd Weed Cannabis Dispensary

Why it matters

Stuffed names often help rankings today, which is why they’re tempting to keep. But any user or competitor can suggest an edit, and Google says unnecessary information in a name can get a profile suspended. A suspension would take a profile holding thousands of reviews off the map.

The fix

Confirm what each storefront sign says, then rename each profile to “Wellgreens” or the exact signage. Keep the neighborhood in the address and on the store page, and let categories, reviews and store pages carry relevance. Stagger the edits rather than changing name, address and categories in one sitting.

02 · Addresses and linksFix now

3 of 10

profiles have an address or link mismatch with your site.

Kearny Mesa’s profile says “8039 Balboa Ave # A”; your homepage says “# B.” The Lake Murray profile name says “Rd”; its address and your site say “Blvd.” Vista’s website field points to thefloraverde.com, which redirects to the Vista page with no tracking tags.

Store count: your site lists 10 stores. A July 2026 Nabis feature says Wellgreens reached 12 California stores by April 2025. If two closed or were sold, their profiles and listings need to be marked closed.

Why it matters

Matching name, address and phone (NAP) across Google, your site, Weedmaps, Leafly and Yelp is a core local trust signal. And without tracking on every profile link, GA4 can’t tell you how many orders start on Google Maps.

The fix

Align suite numbers and street names everywhere. Encinitas already uses the right link pattern (/locations/[store]?utm_source=gmb&utm_medium=organic-[store]). Apply it to all 10, starting with Vista.

03 · Mobile speedBehind

6.9 s

for your homepage’s main content to load on a phone.

Google’s Lighthouse test scores mobile performance 49 out of 100. The page is unresponsive for 1,350 ms while it loads and takes 11.3 seconds to become fully usable. Layout stability is fine (CLS 0.001).

Why it matters

Your buyers search “near me” on a phone, often on the way somewhere. Eleven seconds before the menu works is plenty of time to tap the next result.

The fix

Compress and resize the hero and deals carousel images, defer third-party scripts (the Curator.io Instagram feed is a likely suspect), then re-test.

04 · Blog and resourcesHigh risk

25 posts

target six cities where your site lists no store.

Carlsbad has 7, Oceanside 6 and San Marcos 6, plus paired delivery posts for Del Mar, Solana Beach and Rancho Santa Fe. The resources sitemap was bulk-modified on September 14 and 15, 2026. It also holds five overlapping Comic-Con posts and a typo in a live URL (…weed-delivery-in-visata-ca).

/resources/the-best-dispensary-san-diego competes with your homepage.

/resources/vista-dispensary competes with your Vista store page. Similar posts overlap Mission Valley and both La Mesa stores.

Why it matters

Google’s spam policies name near-identical city pages that funnel visitors to one destination as doorway pages. And every post chasing “best dispensary in San Diego” splits signals with the pages that actually take orders.

The fix

Pull Search Console data, then keep, refresh, merge (301 redirect) or remove each post. Replace the city delivery posts with one strong delivery and service-area page.

05 · Structured data and entityBehind

7 of 16

buyer questions where ChatGPT cites you, your lowest engine.

Your store pages have no LocalBusiness (Store) markup, and their FAQs have no FAQPage markup. Off-site there’s no Google Knowledge Panel, LinkedIn company page, Wikidata entry, Crunchbase profile, press page or named leadership. The only Wikipedia match for your name is Walgreens.

Conflicting facts: the site says “since 2018”; the Nabis feature says 2021. Google’s snippet still shows “Reader Best of 2024,” and Kearny Mesa claims “ranked #1 in San Diego” with no source.

Why it matters

AI engines build their picture of a business from structured data and third-party profiles. With so little of either, ChatGPT has the least to go on, the likeliest reason it trails the other engines. Engines also repeat whichever version of a fact they find first.

The fix

Add Store schema (hours, geo, phone) to each store page and FAQPage to the FAQs you already have. Create LinkedIn, Wikidata and Crunchbase profiles, add an About and press page naming your founders, settle the founding year, source your awards, and write “Wellgreens” as one word everywhere. Add an llms.txt file.

The fix
