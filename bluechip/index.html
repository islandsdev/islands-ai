Start

Where Blue Chip started in July

The program began on a thin foundation. The blog was mostly generic 2021 and 2022 posts, and search was carrying only branded demand. The buyers Blue Chip wants, facility managers choosing a vendor, were not finding the site for the terms they actually search.

  • 122 organic keywords, most of them branded or low intent, and no non-branded commercial term on page one.
  • A thin, dated blog: short, generic posts that did not match an operator voice or speak to vendor selection.
  • AI answers citing the wrong sites: job boards and sibling domains were named for Blue Chip queries ahead of bluechipclean.com.
  • A backlink profile already flagged toxic in Semrush, with Authority Score sitting at 7.
Build

We built a content engine aimed at buyers, not at other cleaners

25 or more posts went live across four pillars, written in a seasoned-operator voice for the facility managers who select cleaning vendors across education, medical, office, industrial, and government sites in the NYC metro.

Pillar 01

Buying & vendor selection

Self-performed versus subcontracted, how to choose a vendor, RFP scope of work, local versus national, superintendent and porter services.

Pillar 02

Cost & budgeting

NYC pricing by facility type, in-house versus outsourced cost and risk, the hidden cost of a dirty building, cleaning myths that cost money.

Pillar 03

Quality & standards

APPA cleaning standards, KPIs, inspections and SLAs, ATP testing, green certifications, cold and flu season disinfection programs.

Pillar 04

Floor care

VCT strip and wax, terrazzo and natural stone, carpet encapsulation versus extraction, autonomous floor scrubbers, slip-and-fall prevention.

The program also showed editorial discipline. An off-brief thread aimed at the wrong audience was caught early and stopped, so the library stayed focused on topics a facility manager actually searches.

An honest note on traffic. Measured organic sessions have not risen yet. Rankings lead traffic by weeks, and a Semrush traffic estimate that spiked sharply in mid-August is a modeled figure tied to the backlink anomaly below, not real visits. The rankings are the real signal. Turning them into traffic depends on the fix in the next section.

The one fix that matters

An off-page backlink problem is capping the results

Authority Score has not moved off 7 while the backlink count more than doubled, from about 1,300 to about 2,500, with referring domains climbing fast and the profile flagged high toxicity. The sudden, low-quality growth looks like link injection rather than anything earned. It puts the new page-one rankings at risk, so it is the priority before more content.

01

Isolate the source

Pull the referring domains added during the spike and check whether they trace to a single network, which separates a bad link vendor from a negative-SEO pattern.

02

Build and submit a disavow

Produce a ready-to-upload disavow file from the toxic set and submit it in Google Search Console, so the junk links stop weighing on the domain.

03

Consolidate brand entity signals
