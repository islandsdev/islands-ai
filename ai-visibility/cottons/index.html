02 · Who the engines think you areHigh risk
0 records

Public knowledge records that describe Cottons the brand. There is no Google Knowledge Panel on a branded search, no LinkedIn company page, no Crunchbase profile and no named founder or leadership anywhere public. The Wikipedia and Wikidata records the audit found under this name are for cotton the crop and for cottonseed oil.

The Wikidata record returned for the name: Q811663, cooking oil extracted from the seeds of cotton plants. That is what a model reads when it tries to resolve “Cottons.”

Why it matters

AI engines build a picture of a business from structured records and third-party profiles, then decide whether it is a real entity worth recommending. With none of them, a common-word brand name resolves to the commodity, and the engine has no reason to put you in an answer about hypo-allergenic pads.

How Islands closes it

Plan 3 builds the entity footprint: a LinkedIn company page, Crunchbase, a Wikidata record for the brand, named leadership, consistent contact details, llms.txt and agents.md. Plan 1 then gives the engines pages worth citing once they know who you are.

Quick polish

Small fixes we’d clear in the first weeks.

  • The homepage meta description repeats itself (“Our Range. From liners and pads…”). Metadata hygiene is the weakest signal in an otherwise strong foundation.
  • No FAQPage markup on the homepage, so the questions you already answer cannot be lifted by an engine.
  • No llms.txt and no agents.md, the two files that tell AI crawlers and agents how to use your content.
  • Image attachment URLs sit in the sitemap as pages, including “applicator-background” and “maternity-pads-background” under /products/, and logo files under /stockists/.
  • The blog archive holds the same screenshot entry three times, plus a privacy notice indexed alongside the articles.
  • Keep the life-stage navigation exactly as it is. Teens, New Mums and Menopause are the strongest structural asset on the site.
