The sequence

The path a call takes.

Three stages, in order. Nothing skips ahead, and nothing reaches a published page without a person approving it.

  1. 01

    Read

    When an article is commissioned, we pull the transcripts of your recent Gong calls, up to a dozen from the last 90 days. Text only. No audio, no video, no recordings leave Gong.

  2. 02

    Extract

    Those transcripts are read once against the specific topic being written, and only the material that serves that topic comes out. A call about ticketing contributes nothing to an article about fundraising. Names, schools and contact details are stripped at this step.

  3. 03

    Write

    The writer receives that evidence alongside the brief, and the same evidence is given to the editor that reviews the draft. The article is checked against the calls it was built from, not just against itself.

Attribution

How call material is credited.

Sales calls are private, so nothing in an article ever points back to one. Evidence from calls is written as what it is, direct experience with customers, in phrases like “the athletic directors we speak with describe...”. It is never presented as research, never footnoted, and never turned into a statistic. If several callers said something, the article says many, not a percentage.

Safeguards

What stays private.

  • ✓

    Read-only access, text only

    The connection can list calls and read transcripts. It cannot change anything in Gong, and it has no access to audio or video.

  • ✓

    Speakers become roles, not names

    Before a transcript is read, every speaker is relabelled by role. The writer sees Prospect and Vendor rep, never a person's name.

  • ✓

    Identifying details are removed twice

    Names of people, schools and districts are excluded during extraction. Email addresses, phone numbers and links are stripped again afterwards as a second pass.

  • ✓

    Nothing publishes itself

    Every article arrives as a draft for review. Publishing is always a person's decision.

What it never does.

  • ×

    Quote a customer by name

    Quotes are used to understand the language, then written as anonymous, representative experience.

  • ×

    Invent a figure from a conversation

    Numbers in an article come from sources that can be checked, never inferred from what someone said on a call.

  • ×

    Share your calls across accounts

    Your call material informs only your own articles. It is never pooled with, or used for, any other business.

  • ×

    Force a term where it does not fit

    If a phrase does not belong in a sentence, it is left out. Natural writing beats jargon-stuffing.
