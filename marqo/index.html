What Claude thinks you are

"What is Marqo?" (asked of the AIs)

Marqo is an AI-native ecommerce search, recommendations and merchandising platform. Instead of off-the-shelf keyword search, it trains dedicated AI models on each merchant's catalog and shopper behavior to power semantic search, image and multimodal search, AI recommendations, automated merchandising and conversational commerce. The pitch is 'Smarter Search, Higher Conversion' and 'Commerce Superintelligence.' It is SOC 2 Type 2 certified, has teams in San Francisco, London and Melbourne, and grew out of a widely used open-source vector search engine. The proof is unusually hard: over $130M in proven revenue for enterprise retailers, with named results like KICKS CREW (+17.7% conversion), Mejuri (+19.8% search revenue per user), MUJI (+19.5% revenue per collection user) and Dooney & Bourke (+27.5% search add-to-cart), and logos including Redbubble, Shutterstock and Poshmark.

Here's the disconnect. Your foundation is as strong as it gets: real enterprise logos, hard revenue numbers ($130M+ proven), SOC 2, an active blog, an open-source following, and content that even ranks in classic Google search (your own 'Best AI Ecommerce Search Platforms' post shows up for that query). But when we asked the AIs the questions your buyers actually ask (best AI ecommerce search platform, best recommendations engine, best Algolia alternative), Marqo never came back. Every answer went to Constructor, Bloomreach, Algolia, Coveo and Dynamic Yield, and Google's own AI Overview named those same incumbents while skipping you. Findable by name, and even ranking your own blog, is not the same as being the platform AI recommends. The reason is fixable: the category is being defined right now by comparison and 'best X for Y' content, and Marqo is not publishing enough answer-shaped content that frames it as THE ecommerce search choice, so the LLMs default to the incumbents. Whoever owns that content owns the recommendation.
